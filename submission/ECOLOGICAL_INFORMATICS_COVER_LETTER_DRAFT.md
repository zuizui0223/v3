Dear Editors,

Please consider our manuscript, **“The event table is not the observation universe: retaining information before record-entry loss in ecological sensing,”** for publication in *Ecological Informatics*.

Automated ecological monitoring increasingly converts continuous or opportunity-based processes into selected event tables. Our manuscript addresses an information-system problem that occurs before downstream ecological inference: useful side information may be discarded, and ecological opportunities may fail to enter the retained table at all. We distinguish these two operations and ask how monitoring systems can preserve and audit the distinctions needed by later ecological analyses.

The paper combines a controlled information-retention result with external empirical observation-process audits. In the controlled system, a correctly time-coupled reference improved balanced utility from 0.5688 to 0.8327 and reduced nuisance false-frame rate from 0.2986 to 0.0272, whereas a time-permuted reference produced only partial benefit. In external camera-trap data, badger representation changed from 0.3598 among 881 independently observed fox/badger passes to 0.4393 after triggering and 0.4828 after confirmed capture. In protected acoustic data, a true late-minus-early contrast of 0.130820 was essentially absent among truth-positive retained entries even under oracle downstream semantics. Entry-aware correction helped in matched contexts but worsened under a frozen camera-plus-position double holdout.

The contribution is an ecological-informatics distinction between the **observation-opportunity universe** and the **retained event table**. A selected table is a measurement product, not automatically the full opportunity universe. We therefore argue for retaining discriminating side information before irreversible entry decisions and for maintaining independently auditable opportunity/reference records when omitted opportunities can alter the ecological estimand.

We believe this fits *Ecological Informatics* because the manuscript is centrally about ecological sensor/data acquisition, information processing, uncertainty and the integrity of monitoring records rather than about one focal taxon or one classifier. The paper does not claim a universally beneficial reference channel, universal detector thresholds, universal correction transport, or population-level independence of camera-trap passes.

All quantitative manuscript claims are tied to pinned source artifacts and reproducible analysis code. BirdVox data are documented under CC BY 4.0. For the Findlay camera-trap source, the open-access article explicitly identifies the public GitHub repository as its online R/data resource; because the repository itself does not display a root licence, our reviewer package will not redistribute the original source CSV files unless repository-level reuse terms are clarified. Instead, reproducibility uses pinned source retrieval, hash verification and derived summaries.
