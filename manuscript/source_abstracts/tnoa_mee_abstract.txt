Automated ecological sensors create detections and non-detections for downstream ecological analyses. Yet a non-detection can reflect absence, inadequate measurement support, nuisance processes, missing attribution or target–nuisance co-occurrence. Collapsing these situations at the sensor interface can irreversibly discard observation-process information.

We develop target–nuisance–observability–abstention (TNOA) as an upstream observation contract. Target and nuisance are positive, non-complementary supports; measurement support is separate; target-coupled responses require attribution; and biological absence requires independent evidence. Using frozen observer generations, we tested TNOA in a registered synthetic design and quantified information loss when B/T/N/U was collapsed to target/not-target. A minimal Python API supports reuse after domain-specific calibration.

After the nuisance representation changed, ranking was retained but an inherited raw threshold failed its registered rule. A predeclared family-conditional false-attribution criterion at alpha=0.05 yielded held-out rates of 0/43,200 and 1,920/43,200 (0.0444). Across 3,003 latent-regime compositions, median compatible target-prevalence width was 0.0299 with B/T/N/U versus 0.2656 after binary collapse. Only 141/3003 compositions had target prevalence <=0.2, but their medians were 0.000175 and 0.0741; under kappa=10 adversarial reweighting, B/T/N/U still removed at least 57.5% of weighted-mean binary width. A post-freeze control showed that finer U splitting was not semantic-specific: 48% of 500 unlabeled two-way splits matched or exceeded the frozen two-reason split. The preregistered matched-timescale ambiguity ridge was not supported.

TNOA's contribution is process-semantic: preserve core observation-process distinctions before binary collapse and calibrate support decisions against explicit error criteria rather than inherited score values. Finer reason provenance should be retained when independently justified, not because extra categories are intrinsically informative. Current results are closed-world rather than field-calibrated; field translation requires independent truth, grouped calibration and frozen held-out evaluation.
